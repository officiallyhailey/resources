The thread, three ways

Same idea in all three: a hairline runs from the end of the section down off the bottom of the screen, and the whole strip is the target. What differs is what the line does. One drops something down it, one gets pulled down it, and one draws itself down. Watch them together, then at half speed.

Compare at
1A

The plumb

The full picture

a section, finished, waiting

What it does. A weighted bead falls the length of the line, lands on a small rest, and recoils once. Slow to start, fastest in the middle, and it stops somewhere.

How it reads. Gravity. It is the only one of the three with a destination built into the motion - something arrives, and the rest mark says "here". Most legible from the corner of the eye.

Cost: the most animated of the three, so it is also the most likely to feel busy on a page this quiet.

1B

The tether

The full picture

a section, finished, waiting

What it does. The cord hangs short, then is tugged down quickly and recovers slowly. The pull is the fast half of the cycle; the return is the slow half you barely notice.

How it reads. Something below wants you. It is the only one that implies a force rather than a path - closest to the brief of "the page is prompting you", because a tug is directed at someone.

Cost: asymmetric timing is doing all the work. Get the ratio wrong and it reads as bouncing, which is the thing we are avoiding.

1C

The pour

The full picture

a section, finished, waiting

What it does. The line is not there to begin with. It draws itself downward like ink running down a page, pools at the floor, holds, and clears.

How it reads. The path is the motion - nothing travels along a route, the route builds itself towards where you are going. The calmest of the three and the one that sits most naturally under serif copy.

Cost: quietest, so also the easiest to miss. Wants the label beside it doing half the work.
