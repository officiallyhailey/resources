Three ways to say keep going

The deck is hard-gated, so this control is the only way forward and it has to read as an instruction rather than as decoration. None of these is a circle that pulses in place: each says "continue" by a different means. All three sit where the current gate sits, hold the mail button's line, and are clickable below.

01

The thread

travel, not throb

The full picture

a section, finished, waiting

What it is. A hairline runs from the end of the section down off the bottom of the screen, and a short bright stroke travels down it, over and over. The line is always there; the movement is always downward.

Why it reads. A pulse says "look at me". A line going somewhere says "this way". It also gives the eye a direction to follow rather than a point to stare at, which is the actual instruction.

The cost: it is subtle. Someone skimming may take a beat longer to spot it than a 58px button. Worth pairing with the label.

02

The page speaks

the sentence is the control

The full picture

a section, finished, waiting

What it is. No button at all. The site carries on talking, in the same typed serif voice as every other line on the page, and the sentence itself is what you click. A rule wipes in beneath it the way a hand underlines a word.

Why it reads. The whole design has trained the reader that typed text is the page addressing them. This turns the gate into the last thing the page says rather than a piece of furniture bolted to the bottom of it.

In your voiceNo new chromeMost on-brand

The cost: text does not look clickable at a glance the way a circle does. Needs the underline, and needs the wording to be an invitation every time - "Where I have worked" works, a bare noun would not.

03

The page beneath

show, don't point

The full picture

a section, finished, waiting

Where I have worked Pull up

What it is. The next section is already there, underneath, and it lifts a sliver of itself over the bottom edge and settles back - the way a page under a page tells you it exists. Hovering lifts it further and holds it. The thing you click is the next section itself.

Why it reads. It never has to say "there is more": it shows the more. It is also the only one of the three that names the destination inside the control, so the reader knows what they are agreeing to before they click.

The cost: the heaviest of the three, and it eats about 70px of the bottom of every panel. On a phone that competes with the mail button, so one of the two would have to move.
